Engagement models

Three ways to bring me in.

Most projects fit one of these cleanly — if yours doesn't, that's exactly what the first call is for.

ONE-TIME

Fixed-Scope Quality Audit

A focused, time-boxed pass over your site or app: functional gaps, accessibility issues, broken flows, and a prioritized write-up of what to fix first.

  • Defined scope and timeline, agreed up front
  • Written report with severity & reproduction steps
  • Good fit for a pre-launch check or a second opinion
FLEXIBLE

Hourly / Contract Testing

Ongoing manual and automated testing support, billed for actual time worked — scale up around a release, scale down between them.

  • No long-term commitment required
  • Manual, automation, or API testing as needed
  • Good fit for short sprints or release crunches
ONGOING

Embedded Sprint QA

Working inside your team's sprint cycle as the QA/SDET function — test planning, automation, defect triage, and release sign-off, sprint after sprint.

  • Attends standups/planning like a team member
  • Builds and maintains your automation suite
  • Good fit for teams without a dedicated QA hire yet

Pricing

Scoped per engagement, not a fixed rate card.

Pricing depends on scope, timeline, and engagement type — a fixed-scope audit and an ongoing embedded role aren't priced the same way, and a rate card would either overstate small projects or understate larger ones.

The honest version: tell me what you need on the discovery call, and you'll get a straight quote before anything is agreed — in writing, no pressure, no obligation to proceed.

FAQ

Common questions before reaching out.

How fast can you start?⌄
Usually within a few days for a fixed-scope audit, and flexible for contract/embedded work depending on current commitments — this gets confirmed on the discovery call, not left vague.
Do you sign NDAs?⌄
Yes — happy to sign an NDA before any access or confidential details are shared.
What does a quality audit actually include?⌄
A structured pass through your product's critical flows: functional bugs, broken or confusing UX, accessibility gaps, and anything else that would embarrass you in front of a customer — delivered as a prioritized, written report with reproduction steps, not a vague "looks fine."
Do you only work with healthcare/ERP/HR products?⌄
No — that's just where recent project experience happens to be. The underlying skill (manual + automated testing, API validation, release-quality judgment) transfers across domains; the discovery call covers anything domain-specific I'd need to get up to speed on.
